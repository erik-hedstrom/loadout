An **appendix** usually contains extra material that supports or expands on the report, such as a table of survey results. An **annex** often contains material that is more self-contained or supplied as a separate supporting document, such as a copy of a form used in the study.

Usage varies by organization and style guide, so this distinction isn’t universal. Since you haven’t provided a required naming convention, a simple rule is: use **appendix** for supporting detail that belongs with the report, and **annex** for a separate supporting document. Either term can work if you use it consistently.

For multiple sections, label them in order—**Appendix A, Appendix B** (or **Annex A, Annex B**)—give each a clear title, and refer to them by the same label in the report, such as “See Appendix A.” Don’t switch terms midway unless your style guide calls for it.

To recommend the best label, I’d need to know what the extra material is, whether it’s part of the report or a separate document, and whether your organization or audience prefers a particular term.
