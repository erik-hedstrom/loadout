# Acquisition target screening framework

This framework is for comparing an initial shortlist, not for approving a transaction. It combines strategic fit, ease of integration, and financial attractiveness while keeping hard disqualifiers outside the weighted score. All names and figures in the worked example are fictional and explicitly hypothetical.

## 1. Run the screen before scoring

Record each requirement as **Pass**, **Fail**, or **Unknown**. A Fail means stop, exclude, or escalate for an explicit exception; do not let a high weighted score compensate for it. Unknown means hold the target from an unconditional ranking until verified. Tailor thresholds to your company, jurisdiction, and deal mandate.

| Nonnegotiable screen | Example evidence / decision rule |
|---|---|
| Legal ability to acquire and operate | No known prohibition, sanctions issue, or unresolvable ownership restriction; confirm counsel review. |
| Strategic mandate | Target fits an approved geography, business, and deal-size mandate. |
| Minimum return hurdle | Base and downside cases can meet the board-approved return / value-creation hurdle at a supportable price. If not yet modeled, mark Unknown. |
| Funding and liquidity | Funding is available and the downside case does not breach approved liquidity, leverage, or covenant limits. |
| Critical rights and liabilities | No known unmanageable litigation, environmental, cybersecurity, licensing, or change-of-control issue. |
| Data integrity and access | Sufficient reliable information and access to validate the business; inability to obtain it is a diligence stop. |

A failed requirement can be reconsidered only if the underlying fact changes or the authorized decision-maker documents an exception. Keep the exception visible.

## 2. Scoring scale and direction

Score every criterion from **1 to 5** using the anchors below. Use whole numbers for screening consistency; add a short evidence note and confidence label. **Higher is always better.** Integration criteria are stated as *ease / manageability*, so a target that is harder to integrate gets a lower score.

| Score | General anchor |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Materially poor; likely value destruction or major unresolved obstacle. |
| 2 | Below expectations; significant gap, cost, or risk. |
| 3 | Meets minimum / mixed; manageable with a credible plan. |
| 4 | Strong; limited gaps and good supporting evidence. |
| 5 | Exceptional; clear advantage with strong, validated evidence. |

Use the criterion-specific anchors below to make judgments comparable. Do not award 4 or 5 based only on seller claims. “Unknown” is not a score: use a provisional score plus low confidence, or mark unscored and show a range. Do not silently substitute a neutral 3.

## 3. Weighted criteria

Weights sum to 100% within each dimension. The default dimension weights are **Strategic fit 35%, Integration ease 30%, Financial attractiveness 35%**. Strategic and financial value are co-equal priorities; integration gets a substantial weight because complexity can delay or prevent value capture. Change these weights only to reflect a stated deal mandate, and show the sensitivity.

### Strategic fit — 35% of total

| Criterion | Weight in dimension | What to assess (1 vs 5) |
|---|---:|---|
| Strategic adjacency | 30% | 1: little connection to core plan; 5: directly advances a named strategic priority with a clear path. |
| Capability / asset gap filled | 25% | 1: limited or replicable assets; 5: scarce capability, IP, data, talent, or channel that would take materially longer to build. |
| Market and customer access | 25% | 1: weak/overlapping access or uncertain demand; 5: credible entry to attractive customers, segments, or geographies. |
| Differentiation and durability | 20% | 1: easily copied or declining position; 5: durable advantage supported by evidence and defensible economics. |

### Integration ease — 30% of total (higher = easier)

| Criterion | Weight in dimension | What to assess (1 vs 5) |
|---|---:|---|
| Systems and process compatibility | 30% | 1: incompatible core platforms/data and extensive migration; 5: compatible stack/processes with limited transition. |
| Talent, culture, and leadership continuity | 25% | 1: high flight / leadership / incentive risk; 5: aligned operating style and credible retention plan. |
| Operating / regulatory integration | 25% | 1: complex licenses, safety, quality, or control environment; 5: low burden and clear integration path. |
| Standalone dependencies and separation | 20% | 1: tangled shared services, carve-out, or supplier/customer dependencies; 5: self-contained with clear ownership and contracts. |

Score **difficulty and execution exposure**, not dollar costs, here. Put one-time integration costs in the financial model once. A target can be expensive to integrate but technically straightforward, or cheap but operationally difficult; distinguish those cases.

### Financial attractiveness — 35% of total

| Criterion | Weight in dimension | What to assess (1 vs 5) |
|---|---:|---|
| Price / valuation discipline | 25% | 1: price materially exceeds supportable value / return limits; 3: at hurdle; 5: compelling value at a defensible price. Compare enterprise value and equity value on consistent debt/cash and working-capital definitions. |
| Standalone cash generation and returns | 30% | 1: weak/volatile cash conversion and poor returns; 3: acceptable standalone case; 5: strong, durable cash generation and returns before buyer synergies. |
| Net synergy value and capture confidence | 20% | 1: little credible net value; 3: moderate, evidenced opportunity; 5: material, time-phased and owner-backed opportunity after costs and leakage. |
| Downside resilience | 25% | 1: severe impairment or funding strain in downside; 3: downside is survivable and within risk limits; 5: resilient cash flows, low concentration/contingency exposure, ample headroom. |

### Calculation

For each dimension, calculate the weighted average of its criterion scores. Then:

`Total score (out of 5) = 0.35 × Strategic + 0.30 × Integration ease + 0.35 × Financial`

Equivalent 100-point score = total × 20. Retain unrounded values for ranking and display two decimals. A small score difference is not meaningful when evidence is weak; report score ranges and confidence alongside the rank.

## 4. Handle price, synergies, costs, uncertainty, and downside once

Use a common valuation date, currency, forecast period, financing convention, and definition of enterprise value for every target.

- **Price:** Use the price actually contemplated, including assumed debt, deferred/contingent consideration, fees where relevant, and normalized working capital. The price criterion reflects whether that price is attractive. Also use that same price in the return and downside models; do not add a separate “cheap price” bonus elsewhere.
- **Standalone value:** Forecast target cash flows without buyer-specific synergies. Score standalone quality separately from synergy opportunity so a weak business is not made to look strong by speculative benefits.
- **Synergies:** Build a bottom-up bridge by initiative, owner, gross benefit, timing, probability, dis-synergies, taxes, and leakage. Score net value/capture after one-time integration costs, recurring costs, taxes, and delay. Do not score gross synergy in one row and again in standalone returns. Avoid adding synergy value to both a valuation multiple comparison and a separate score without reconciling the models.
- **Integration costs:** Estimate one-time and recurring costs by workstream and timing. Include these in net synergy value / acquisition cash flows and the return model once. Integration ease scores execution complexity, not the same cost estimate. If a difficulty item is already represented in modeled delay or cost, do not impose another unsubstantiated financial penalty for it.
- **Downside:** Model a coherent downside case (e.g., revenue shortfall, margin pressure, delayed synergies, higher costs, and financing headroom) together. The downside-resilience score should reflect the result of that case. Do not separately subtract the same downside in another criterion.
- **Uncertainty:** For each key input, record source, date, confidence (High / Medium / Low), and plausible range. Use scenario or probability-weighted values only where probabilities have a defensible basis. Otherwise show low/base/high cases and a score range. Low-confidence claims should not receive a high score merely because the forecast is precise. For shortlist ranking, flag any target whose plausible range crosses another target’s score; resolve uncertainty in diligence.
- **No double counting:** Keep a benefits and risks ledger mapping each item to exactly one model line and one score rationale. Financial criterion scores summarize the model; they are not extra cash flows. If strategic value is not monetized in the financial model, describe it in strategic fit; do not capitalize it again as synergy without evidence.

## 6. Worked comparison (all figures hypothetical)

Fictional targets: **Aster**, **Beacon**, and **Cinder**. Scores below are provisional screening judgments. The example assumes each target passes the initial nonnegotiable screen; that assumption itself requires verification. Currency is fictional “$”; valuation figures are illustrative only and are not market evidence.

| Target | Hypothetical headline inputs | Strategic scores (adjacency, capability, access, durability) | Integration ease scores (systems, people, operating, dependencies) | Financial scores (price, standalone, net synergies, downside) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aster | EV $420m; standalone value range $380–460m; 5-year standalone IRR 13%; buyer hurdle 12%; net synergy PV $45m after $30m one-time costs; downside IRR 7%, no assumed covenant breach. | 4.5, 4, 4, 3.5 | 3, 4, 4, 3 | 3, 4, 3, 4 |
| Beacon | EV $300m; standalone value range $320–390m; 5-year standalone IRR 16%; net synergy PV $25m after $18m costs; downside IRR 9%, headroom remains. | 3.5, 4, 4.5, 4 | 4, 3.5, 3, 4 | 4, 4, 4, 3.5 |
| Cinder | EV $500m; standalone value range $450–560m; 5-year standalone IRR 14%; net synergy PV $100m after $70m costs; downside IRR 3%, concentrated customer and integration exposure. | 4.5, 4.5, 3, 4.5 | 2, 2.5, 3, 2 | 3, 4.5, 4.5, 2.5 |

“Standalone value” is illustrative enterprise value before buyer synergies. Net synergy PV is separate and already net of the stated one-time integration costs; it is not added again to standalone value in the scorecard. IRR figures are hypothetical, illustrative model outputs and should be recalculated using a consistent transaction model.

| Target | Strategic subtotal | Integration ease subtotal | Financial subtotal | Total / 100 | Initial rank |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Aster | 4.05 | 3.45 | 3.40 | 3.64 / 72.85 | 2 |
| Beacon | 3.98 | 3.63 | 3.88 | 3.84 / 76.70 | 1 |
| Cinder | 4.13 | 2.38 | 3.63 | 3.43 / 68.50 | 3 |

Calculations use unrounded subtotals: Aster = 0.35×4.05 + 0.30×3.45 + 0.35×3.40 = 3.6425; Beacon = 3.835; Cinder = 3.425. Beacon leads because it combines a reasonable strategic fit with easier execution and stronger price/standalone economics. Cinder has the strongest strategic and synergy scores but its low integration ease and downside resilience pull it down. If a screen fails, that target is excluded or held regardless of this ranking.

## 7. Sensitivity checks

These are illustrative ranking tests, not forecasts. Recalculate after changing weights or input scores; do not adjust one assumption in isolation if it should flow through the financial model.

| Case | Strategic / Integration / Financial weights | Aster | Beacon | Cinder | Ranking |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Default | 35 / 30 / 35 | 3.64 | 3.82 | 3.42 | Beacon, Aster, Cinder |
| Strategy-led mandate | 50 / 20 / 30 | 3.74 | 3.88 | 3.63 | Beacon, Aster, Cinder |
| Return-led mandate | 25 / 25 / 50 | 3.58 | 3.84 | 3.44 | Beacon, Aster, Cinder |
| Beacon repriced: valuation score falls 4 → 1 | 35 / 30 / 35; Beacon financial subtotal falls 3.88 → 3.13 | 3.64 | 3.57 | 3.42 | Aster, Beacon, Cinder |
| Cinder synergies less credible: net synergy score falls 4.5 → 2.5 | 35 / 30 / 35; Cinder financial subtotal falls 3.63 → 3.23 | 3.64 | 3.84 | 3.29 | Beacon, Aster, Cinder |

The two repricing / synergy cases show why shortlist rank must be revisited as diligence changes price and evidence. For Beacon, the valuation criterion change alone reduces the total by 0.2625 points; an actual reprice should also update IRR and downside scenarios, which may move other financial scores. For Cinder, a two-point haircut to net-synergy confidence reduces total by 0.14. Where a changed assumption affects multiple model outputs, update the model coherently and avoid applying an additional manual score penalty for the same effect.

## 8. What to gather before serious candidacy

1. **Mandate and screens:** approved strategic thesis, geographic / sector boundaries, return hurdle, leverage and liquidity limits, regulatory constraints, and explicit disqualifiers.
2. **Price and deal perimeter:** seller expectations, EV-to-equity bridge, debt-like items, cash, normalized working capital, contingent payments, assumed liabilities, carve-out perimeter, and transaction costs.
3. **Quality of earnings and cash:** at least three to five years of audited statements where available, monthly results, revenue and margin by product/customer/region, cash conversion, capex, working capital seasonality, debt, and forecast-to-actual history.
4. **Customer and market evidence:** customer concentration, retention/churn, contract terms, backlog quality, pricing, market size and growth, competitive position, channel overlap, and independent customer references where permitted.
5. **Synergy proof:** initiative-level owner, baseline, gross benefit, timing, probability, required investment, dis-synergies, taxes, leakage, dependencies, and a plan to track realization.
6. **Integration facts:** systems architecture and data quality, process maps, key-person dependencies, retention needs, culture/decision rights, facilities, suppliers, transitional services, cyber posture, and a realistic integration sequence.
7. **Legal and risk diligence:** corporate ownership, IP chain of title, litigation, compliance, licenses, privacy, cybersecurity incidents, environmental / safety matters, tax, labor, contracts, change-of-control rights, and insurance.
8. **Downside and financing:** customer loss or demand shock cases, synergy delay / failure, cost overruns, working-capital needs, financing terms, covenant headroom, liquidity runway, and exit / impairment assumptions.
9. **Evidence quality:** source and date for every material input, management incentives, accounting policy changes, data gaps, and independent validation of the assumptions most capable of changing the rank.

Treat a target as serious only after the screens are verified, the price and perimeter are sufficiently defined, the standalone and downside cases meet mandate, and the largest rank-sensitive assumptions have credible evidence and named owners.
