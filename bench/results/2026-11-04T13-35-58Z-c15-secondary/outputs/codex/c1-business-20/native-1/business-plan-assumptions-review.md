# Business plan assumptions review

**Purpose:** identify what to learn before spending substantially on product development.

## Sample business plan used

No plan or other source material was present in the folder, so this review uses a realistic example to demonstrate the method. Replace these details with your actual plan before acting on the conclusions.

**Customer:** owner-operators and office managers at small residential HVAC service businesses (roughly 3–20 technicians) in one metro area.

**Problem:** inbound service inquiries and quote requests are missed or followed up late when staff are busy, leaving potential jobs unbooked.

**Proposed solution:** a lightweight service that captures web and missed-call inquiries, sends prompt text follow-up, and helps staff book an appointment. Initially it could be delivered with existing messaging and scheduling tools plus manual setup; no custom product is assumed.

**Pricing hypothesis:** $199 per location per month, with a one-time $300 setup fee.

**Distribution hypothesis:** founder-led outreach to local HVAC firms, referrals from trade consultants, and partnerships with small marketing agencies serving contractors.

**Cost hypotheses:** messaging/software below $30 per customer monthly; onboarding below two staff-hours; support below one hour per customer monthly. Founder sales time and acquisition cost are not yet estimated.

**Evidence available in this sample:** none. Every market, product, price, and cost statement above is a hypothesis, not observed evidence. Compliments, survey enthusiasm, and stated intent would not count as purchase evidence.

## Recommended testing order

1. **Problem evidence:** complete the 12 discovery interviews. Seek recent behavior and quantified impact, not opinions about the concept.
2. **Payment:** make a real, specific paid pilot offer at the hypothesized price. Do this before investing in custom software.
3. **Access:** run a measured outreach sprint alongside the pilot offers so reachability and sales effort are visible.
4. **Concierge feasibility:** deliver the workflow manually to paying pilot customers and log failures, response times, and staff effort.
5. **Unit costs:** use actual delivery time and vendor charges to assess margin; include founder labor rather than treating it as free.
6. **Retention:** charge for a second month and inspect actual repeated usage. Do not infer retention from a successful demo or first-week activity.
7. **Broader channel tests:** only after direct sales show payment and recurring value, test referral partners.

## Evidence rules for this review

- **Evidence:** observed behavior, records of recent incidents, actual payment/deposit, measured workflow performance, logged costs, or renewal.
- **Belief:** projections, founder intuition, compliments, survey intent, unpriced waitlists, and verbal willingness to buy. These may guide experiments but do not validate purchasing behavior.
- Treat thresholds as decision rules for this illustrative case, not universal benchmarks. If a test misses, inspect the reason before changing several assumptions at once.

## Before substantial development

For this sample, do not authorize a custom build until there is evidence of a frequent costly problem, at least three real paid pilot commitments at the target price, a workable manual service path, and measured costs compatible with the price. Then build only the repeated, costly manual steps observed in the pilots.
