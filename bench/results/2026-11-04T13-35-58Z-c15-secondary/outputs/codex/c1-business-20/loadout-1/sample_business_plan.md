# Illustrative sample business plan: ShiftNote

> **Synthetic example only.** The user supplied no plan or market evidence. Every company detail, number, and proposed threshold below is invented to demonstrate the method; none should be read as research findings or a recommendation about a real business.

## One-paragraph plan

ShiftNote is a proposed lightweight booking follow-up tool for independent residential cleaning companies with 5–30 cleaners. It would help an office manager recover quote requests and prompt past customers to rebook, using SMS and a simple job calendar. The assumed problem is that leads and repeat appointments are lost when follow-up is handled manually across calls, texts, and spreadsheets. The proposed buyer is the owner or office manager. The assumed price is $99 per business per month, with a 14-day trial. Initial distribution would be founder-led direct outreach to local cleaning companies and referrals from bookkeeping or cleaning-industry partners. A first version might use existing messaging and calendar services; assume $8,000–$15,000 to build a usable MVP and ongoing software/support costs of $20–$35 per account monthly before founder compensation and acquisition costs.

## What is known versus assumed

- **Provided evidence:** none. No customer interviews, observed workflows, usage data, purchase commitments, supplier quotes, or cost model were supplied.
- **Synthetic planning inputs:** customer segment, pain description, product, price, channel, costs, and thresholds in this example.
- **Beliefs to test:** companies lose enough revenue to prioritize this; they can be reached economically; they will pay $99/month; the workflow can be integrated and delivered reliably; support and messaging costs leave margin; customers keep using it after the first few weeks.

## Assumed economics to investigate

At $99/month and $20–$35 direct monthly delivery cost, the illustrative gross margin would be about 65%–80% before payment fees, acquisition, onboarding, support labor, and overhead. Those direct cost figures are guesses, not quotes. If onboarding is high touch, messaging volumes are large, or churn is high, the economics may fail even if customers like the product.
