# Vendor quote comparison — synthetic test run

Prepared 7 October 2026. **All three vendors and quotes are fictional. No real quotes were supplied.** Source inputs: [Atlas](sample-quotes/atlas.md), [Boreal](sample-quotes/boreal.md), and [Cedar](sample-quotes/cedar.md). This recommendation applies only to these sample inputs.

Basis: ten operating commercial air purifiers at one site, equivalent quoted capacity and filtration, three years of ownership. **Total cost of ownership is the primary priority**, since no priorities were supplied. Currency is SEK throughout; no exchange rate is needed. Primary comparison excludes tax; tax-inclusive cash costs use the 25% rate explicitly specified in these fictional quotes, not an independently determined tax treatment. Cedar requires buying twelve units, with two retained as unused spares.

The arithmetic is initial cost + three annual filter charges + any three annual subscription charges. Atlas: 4,200 + 180 + 300 + 500 + 3 × (600 + 240) = 7,700. Boreal: 5,900 ÷ 1.25 + 3 × 750 = 6,970. Cedar: 12 × 450 + 250 + 200 + 3 × 500 = 7,350, before unknown costs. See [cost breakdown CSV](cost-breakdown.csv).

**Trade-offs and factor winners.** Boreal has the lowest delivered/installed price and the lowest three-year planned TCO: 730 less than Atlas and at least 380 less than Cedar, excluding tax. Atlas has the lowest hardware-only price, but fees and its required subscription reverse that advantage. Boreal offers the best quoted usable-delivery commitment; Cedar dispatches sooner but provides no arrival or installation date. Boreal also has the longest and broadest warranty. Boreal has the clearest cost terms, no required subscription, and favorable payment timing; Atlas introduces subscription lock-in and Cedar leaves several charges open.

**Overall recommendation: choose Boreal provisionally**, subject to confirming performance equivalence and service commitments. It wins each requested factor on the stated operating basis, although its filter cost is the highest. Its 36-month on-site warranty also reduces unpriced repair exposure. The 380 margin against Cedar is narrow, but Cedar is already more expensive before its unquoted installation charges. Do not treat a five-day dispatch promise as faster usable delivery.

The recommendation assumes all ten units meet the same required performance, the quoted fixed prices remain available within validity dates, three years of operation, one replacement filter per active unit each year, and ten operating units even when twelve are purchased. We budget three replacement rounds conservatively; ask whether the third replacement falls outside the intended ownership window. Buying only two replacement rounds preserves the ranking: Atlas 6,860, Boreal 6,220, Cedar 6,850 plus unknowns. Figures are undiscounted; tax-exclusive ranking assumes tax is recoverable, while the cash column supports comparison if it is not. Equal tax treatment preserves the ranking.

Electricity, staff maintenance time, disposal, downtime, and out-of-warranty repair costs are unknown and excluded from all three totals. Their equality is unverified, so these are planned quoted-cost comparisons rather than complete lifetime-cost forecasts. Boreal's advantage over Atlas disappears if Boreal incurs over 730 more in these excluded costs; its advantage over Cedar disappears if Boreal incurs over 380 more plus Cedar's unknown charges. Supplier reliability and financial stability have not been assessed from these synthetic quotes.

Negotiation priorities: preserve Boreal's inclusive price while obtaining a written delay remedy and repair response time; ask Atlas to remove subscription lock-in; ask Cedar for a ten-unit, fully installed quote and written refund terms.
