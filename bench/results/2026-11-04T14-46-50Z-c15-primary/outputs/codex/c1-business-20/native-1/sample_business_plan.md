# Sample business plan (fictional test input)

> This is a deliberately invented example, created because no plan or data was provided. It is not a claim about the user's business.

## Concise plan

- **Customer:** Independent coffee shops with one to three locations and no dedicated inventory manager.
- **Problem:** Owners spend time checking stock and placing supplier orders; they sometimes run out of key items or over-order perishables.
- **Proposed solution:** A lightweight web app where staff log deliveries and daily stock counts. It recommends reorder quantities and creates supplier-ready order lists. Initial version uses manual data entry; no supplier integrations.
- **Pricing:** $79 per location per month, with a 30-day trial.
- **Distribution:** Founder-led outreach to local cafes, referrals from bookkeepers and coffee equipment/supply vendors, then content and paid search.
- **Costs:** Estimated $25,000 for a first production version; hosting and support estimated at $12 per location monthly, excluding founder time, onboarding, payment fees, and sales costs.
- **Current evidence (fictional):** Founder spoke with 8 cafe owners. Six called inventory tracking a frustration; four said they would “try” a tool. No one paid, signed a pilot agreement, or shared operating data. A clickable spreadsheet prototype was tried by 2 owners for one week; they completed one stock count each, but neither continued using it. No measured time savings, avoided stockouts, or retention data.

## Evidence versus belief

**Observed in this sample:** Some interviewees described the task as frustrating; two people completed one prototype exercise; there are no purchases or repeat-use observations.

**Beliefs still to test:** The problem is frequent and costly enough to motivate change; $79/month is acceptable; reachable buyers can be acquired cheaply; users will enter reliable data; the recommendations can work across supplier formats; support and onboarding fit the cost estimate; recurring use persists after novelty wears off.

Interview compliments, hypothetical “would try” answers, and a single prototype session are signals for further testing, not evidence of purchase or retention.
