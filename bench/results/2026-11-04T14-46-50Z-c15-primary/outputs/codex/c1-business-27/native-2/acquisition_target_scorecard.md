# Acquisition target shortlist scorecard

This framework is for comparing targets at an early screening stage. It supports discussion and prioritization; it does not replace valuation, legal review, or investment approval. All names and numbers in the worked example are fictional and hypothetical.

## 1. Apply nonnegotiable screens first

Record each screen as **Pass / Fail / Unknown**, with evidence and an owner. A Fail means stop, defer, or seek an explicitly approved exception; do not let a high weighted score offset it. Unknown means the target is not yet cleared: obtain the missing evidence before advancing beyond a provisional shortlist.

| Screen | Example pass condition (adapt to buyer policy) |
|---|---|
| Strategic mandate | Fits approved markets, products, geography, and deal-size mandate. |
| Legal ability to acquire | No known ownership, regulatory, sanctions, foreign-investment, or change-of-control barrier that makes the deal infeasible. |
| Integrity and conduct | No unresolved fraud, corruption, serious safety, or other prohibited conduct issue. |
| Minimum economics | A credible base case can meet the buyer's minimum return hurdle at a supportable price, without relying on uncontracted synergies. |
| Funding and liquidity | Purchase and integration funding fit approved financing capacity and liquidity limits. |
| Minimum diligence access | Seller can provide sufficient access to validate ownership, financials, material liabilities, and operations before commitment. |
| Concentration / risk limits | Deal does not breach a hard counterparty, customer, country, leverage, or other portfolio limit. |

Set company-specific thresholds before scoring. These are examples, not universal legal or financial standards. A screen failure and its rationale should remain visible in the decision record even if later cured.

## 2. Scoring scale and calculation

Use the same anchored 1–5 scale for every criterion. Score evidence, not enthusiasm. For integration criteria, **5 always means easier / lower difficulty**, so lower integration difficulty raises the total score.

| Score | Anchor |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Very poor: materially below the buyer's acceptable range; major adverse evidence. |
| 2 | Weak: meaningful gaps or obstacles; improvement depends on uncertain actions. |
| 3 | Adequate / mixed: acceptable with manageable gaps; evidence is incomplete or balanced. |
| 4 | Strong: clearly favorable; limited gaps with a credible path to address them. |
| 5 | Excellent: unusually favorable and well evidenced; little material execution concern. |

For each criterion, multiply its **weight in percentage points** by its score divided by 5. Sum the weighted points for a total from 0 to 100. Missing evidence is not automatically a 3: use the uncertainty method in section 5. Keep criterion weights fixed across targets in a comparison set. A total is a screening aid, not a probability of success.

## 3. Weighted criteria

Suggested default weights reflect a buyer that values durable strategic relevance and risk-adjusted economics while recognizing that integration execution can destroy value. Set and approve weights before seeing target scores. Weights sum to 100.

| Dimension / criterion | Weight | What to assess; 5 means… |
|---|---:|---|
| **Strategic fit (35)** |||
| Strategic alignment | 12 | Directly advances stated strategic priorities; limited rationale drift. |
| Market attractiveness and position | 9 | Attractive, durable market and defensible target position, supported by evidence. |
| Capability / product complementarity | 8 | Fills a clear capability gap or complements the portfolio with little overlap or cannibalization. |
| Customer and channel fit | 6 | Customer needs, routes to market, and brand positioning complement the buyer. |
| **Integration ease (25)** |||
| Operating model and culture compatibility | 7 | Decision rights, incentives, leadership, and working norms can coexist with low disruption. |
| Systems, data, and process integration | 7 | Systems are compatible, data usable, and migration scope limited. |
| People and change execution | 5 | Critical talent is retainable; change load and labor constraints are manageable. |
| Operational / regulatory integration burden | 6 | Few complex site, license, quality, safety, or control changes are required. |
| **Financial attractiveness (40)** |||
| Risk-adjusted return at proposed price | 16 | Buyer case clears its hurdle with margin of safety after price, costs, taxes, and risk; use standalone cash flows plus separately evidenced synergies. |
| Standalone cash-flow quality | 8 | Cash conversion, earnings quality, and working-capital needs are strong and repeatable. |
| Balance sheet and contingent liabilities | 6 | Debt, pensions, tax, litigation, environmental, and other exposures are limited and understood. |
| Downside resilience | 6 | Value and liquidity hold up under plausible adverse scenarios; limited cyclicality or concentration. |
| Capital intensity and funding burden | 4 | Ongoing capex, working capital, and financing needs are modest relative to buyer capacity. |

### What belongs in each dimension

- Strategic fit measures *why this asset belongs in the portfolio* and the quality of the market / complementarity. Do not award points here for the dollar value of the same synergies counted in the financial case.
- Integration ease measures effort, time, and execution risk, with high scores for low burden. Quantified one-time integration costs belong in the financial return calculation; the operational causes of those costs can still inform integration-ease scores. Do not subtract the same cost twice from the total score or financial model.
- Financial attractiveness measures buyer economics. Model the price and cash flows explicitly; do not give a second financial score for generic “synergy potential.” The financial criteria capture returns and risk, not headline seller projections.

## 5. Uncertainty, price, synergies, costs, and downside

### Uncertain or incomplete information

For each score, record a confidence rating and the fact that would change it. Use **High** for verified, current primary evidence; **Medium** for partial corroboration; **Low** for seller claims, proxies, or material gaps. For initial sorting, calculate a provisional point estimate and an uncertainty range: score low-confidence criteria one point lower and one point higher (bounded by 1 and 5), then recompute the total. Show the range, not just the midpoint. If a critical gate is Unknown, mark the candidate provisional regardless of total. Do not disguise missing data as an average score.

For major economic inputs, build a base, downside, and upside case with explicit assumptions. Apply a probability-weighted expected value only when scenario probabilities have a defensible basis; otherwise show the cases separately. Do not mechanically apply an arbitrary “confidence discount” to a target's whole score: uncertainty is criterion-specific and should focus diligence.

### Acquisition price and financial return

Use enterprise value / equity value consistently, bridge debt-like items and cash, and state whether the offer is cash-free / debt-free. Calculate buyer returns from cash paid and timing of cash flows, taxes, financing as relevant, and exit assumptions. Score return **at the proposed price**, and show the maximum price that meets the buyer's hurdle. Re-run the score at that price and at plausible bid changes. A high-quality company can be a poor acquisition at an excessive price.

### Synergies and integration costs without double counting

Maintain a bridge from standalone value to buyer value: (1) target standalone cash flows; (2) incremental revenue / cost synergies by initiative, owner, timing, probability, and dis-synergies; (3) one-time integration costs and required capital; (4) taxes, transaction costs, and financing effects; (5) purchase price. Count only incremental buyer cash flows versus the no-deal baseline. Include implementation ramp, leakage, and ongoing costs. Do not include synergies in standalone value and add them again, count the same cross-sell in two initiatives, or count integration costs both as a cash-flow deduction and a separate financial penalty. Integration burden is separately scored as execution difficulty, not as a duplicate dollar charge. Track realized synergy against the initiative ledger after close.

Use at least a conservative case with no uncontracted revenue synergies and slower / lower cost synergies. Treat seller forecasts as hypotheses until independently supported. A price premium justified by synergies should be compared with the buyer's retained share of those synergies after costs and risk.

### Downside risk

Stress revenue, margin, retention, working capital, capex, integration timing/cost, rates / FX as applicable, and material liabilities. Include correlated shocks where plausible (for example, customer loss plus delayed integration). Financial score inputs should reflect these cases through risk-adjusted return, resilience, and liability quality. Avoid a second generic “risk haircut” after already lowering cash flows for the same risk. Show the downside case, liquidity need, and break-even price explicitly.

Cedar's easier integration and stronger standalone cash profile outweigh Northstar's stronger capability complementarity. Northstar has only a small illustrative value cushion and a systems-migration issue. Harbor's market score is offset by difficult integration, a high price, and an unresolved gate.

## 7. Sensitivity checks

### Change the priority weights

Reweight within dimensions but keep total weight at 100. The table compares the base default with two plausible buyer priorities; scores are recalculated from the same criterion scores.

| Weight set | Strategic / Integration / Financial | Northstar | Cedar | Harbor | Order |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Default | 35 / 25 / 40 | 74.8 | 77.6 | 64.2 | Cedar, Northstar, Harbor |
| Growth-led (strategic emphasized) | 50 / 20 / 30 | 78.5 | 78.2 | 68.1 | Northstar, Cedar, Harbor |
| Execution / return-led | 25 / 30 / 45 | 72.0 | 77.3 | 61.3 | Cedar, Northstar, Harbor |

Growth-led dimension totals use the same within-dimension criterion proportions, scaled to the revised dimension weight. This shows Cedar's lead is sensitive to strategic emphasis; it is not a robust separation. The buyer should decide which priority reflects its approved mandate before scoring.

### Price and synergy stress (same scores except return-at-price criterion)

For illustration, assume the risk-adjusted return score changes by one point for each case below, with all other scores held constant. One score point on this criterion changes the total by 3.2 points (weight 16 ÷ 5). These are scenario assumptions, not forecasts.

| Scenario | Northstar score | Cedar score | Harbor score | Effect |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Base | 74.8 | 77.6 | 64.2 | Cedar first. |
| Northstar price rises 10%, return score 4→3 | 71.6 | 77.6 | 64.2 | Cedar lead widens. |
| Cedar price rises 10%, return score 4→3 | 74.8 | 74.4 | 64.2 | Northstar moves narrowly first. |
| Northstar independently validates $15m more synergy NPV, return score 4→5 | 78.0 | 77.6 | 64.2 | Northstar moves narrowly first. |
| Cedar integration estimate doubles from $12m to $24m; return score 4→3 | 74.8 | 74.4 | 64.2 | Ranking narrows; integration score should also be revisited if the new estimate signals greater execution difficulty. |
| Harbor consent fails | 74.8 | 77.6 | Excluded | Gate overrides weighted ranking. |

These discrete score shifts demonstrate the method, not a valuation formula. In actual work, revise the underlying model and score consistently. Revisit integration-ease scores only when new facts change execution burden; do not also impose an arbitrary score penalty for the same dollar cost. Record what evidence causes each change. A small score gap (for example, under 3 points) is best treated as a practical tie pending diligence, not as meaningful precision.

## 8. What to gather before treating a target as serious

First validate the screens and the assumptions most likely to change the ranking. Request, subject to appropriate confidentiality and process controls:

1. **Ownership and feasibility:** cap table, beneficial owners, corporate structure, material contracts with change-of-control clauses, licenses, permits, regulatory filings, sanctions / integrity checks, litigation, and required consents.
2. **Financial quality:** audited statements for at least three years if available, recent monthly results, revenue and gross margin by product / customer / region, EBITDA-to-cash bridge, working capital seasonality, capex, debt, leases, pensions, tax exposures, and quality-of-earnings support.
3. **Price and deal perimeter:** seller expectations, cash/debt and debt-like items, normalized working capital, excluded assets / liabilities, earn-out or rollover expectations, transaction costs, and a buyer hurdle-rate model with maximum supportable price.
4. **Market and strategic thesis:** market sizing and growth sources, competitor and substitute map, win/loss data, customer concentration and churn, product roadmap, IP ownership / freedom to operate, channel economics, and a clear no-deal baseline.
5. **Synergy proof:** initiative-level owner, baseline, incremental revenue or cost, timing, probability, investment, dependencies, dis-synergies, customer evidence, and a method to avoid overlap. Separate committed / contracted benefits from speculative upside.
6. **Integration reality:** systems inventory and technical debt, data quality / privacy / cybersecurity, process maps, site footprint, operational dependencies, integration workplan, named accountable leaders, change capacity, one-time and recurring costs, and critical-path timing.
7. **People and culture:** leadership and key-person dependency, retention risks, compensation and incentive design, labor / works council requirements, employee liabilities, and culture evidence from more than management interviews.
8. **Downside and resilience:** customer / supplier concentration, backlog quality, cyclicality, price sensitivity, margin bridge, stress cases, contingent liabilities, insurance, business continuity, and cash / liquidity need under delay or underperformance.

Before advancing, identify the three assumptions with the largest effect on value or feasibility, assign an owner and due date to each, and define a stop / reprice / proceed threshold. Re-score after evidence arrives, preserve the original score and rationale, and show exactly which facts caused the rank to move.
