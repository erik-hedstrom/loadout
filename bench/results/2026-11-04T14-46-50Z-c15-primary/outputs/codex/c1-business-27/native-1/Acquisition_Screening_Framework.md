# Acquisition target screening framework

*Illustrative screening aid; not investment, tax, legal, or accounting advice. All company names and figures below are fictional and explicitly hypothetical.*

## How to use it

1. Apply the pass/fail gates first. A failed gate means **stop, reject, or hold pending a defined cure**; do not compute a compensating “good” total.
2. Score each surviving target against the common 1–5 anchors below. Use evidence-based estimates, not false precision.
3. Calculate weighted scores by dimension and overall (0–100). Treat the rank as a shortlist order, not an acquisition decision.
4. Record evidence, confidence, owner, and open questions beside each score. Refresh scores as diligence replaces assumptions with facts.

The attached `Acquisition_Scorecard_Template.csv` is an editable blank scorecard with one row per criterion and fields for ratings, evidence, confidence, assumptions, and follow-up. Use the worked example and sensitivity table below as a realistic filled example; calculate weighted scores using the formulas in this guide.

## 1. Nonnegotiable screening gates

Define thresholds before reviewing targets; tailor them to your mandate and jurisdiction. Examples:

| Gate | Example test | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic mandate | Target is in an approved market, geography, and scope | Fail = reject or explicit mandate exception |
| Legal ability to own/operate | No known legal prohibition, required license barrier, or unmanageable sanctions/export issue | Fail = reject; unresolved = hold |
| Integrity and conduct | No credible evidence of fraud, bribery, material sanctions evasion, or knowingly falsified reporting | Fail = reject; investigate credible allegations |
| Minimum economics | Meets pre-set size/scale floor and has a plausible path to the buyer’s minimum return hurdle at a defensible price | Fail = reject; do not use speculative synergies to pass |
| Funding / transaction feasibility | Financeable within approved capital and leverage limits; transaction can close within mandate | Fail = reject or redesign |
| Critical asset viability | Core IP, permits, customer rights, or key contracts can transfer or remain usable after change of control | Fail = reject; unresolved = hold |
| Unacceptable downside | No identified single exposure exceeds the buyer’s explicit risk appetite (e.g., catastrophic environmental liability) | Fail = reject, ring-fence, or require a verified remedy before proceeding |

A “hold” is not a pass: specify what evidence or remedy would clear it, who owns it, and a deadline. Gate outcomes should be auditable and not embedded as weighted points.

## 2. Scoring scale and direction

Use integer ratings 1–5. For every weighted criterion, **5 is always favorable**. Score against the dimension-specific anchors below; 3 means acceptable/uncertain midpoint, not “average company.” Use N/A only when genuinely irrelevant and reallocate that criterion’s weight within its dimension before comparing targets.

Integration difficulty is deliberately scored as **ease**: 5 = easy / low difficulty; 1 = very difficult / high difficulty. If assessors first rate raw difficulty, invert consistently: ease = 6 − difficulty. Thus lower difficulty improves the score.

Evidence confidence is separate from performance: High = independently verified / audited; Medium = management-supported with partial corroboration; Low = unverified, incomplete, or highly assumption-dependent. Do not multiply the performance score mechanically by confidence: that would systematically punish uncertain upside and can obscure risk. Instead show a range and use a conservative score where uncertainty is decision-relevant.

## 3. Criteria, anchors, weights, and rationale

Weights sum to 100%: strategic fit 40%, integration ease 30%, financial attractiveness 30%. This favors durable strategic rationale while giving substantial weight to execution and returns. Change weights to match the buyer’s stated strategy, then show the alternative weighting in sensitivity analysis.

### Strategic fit — 40%

| Criterion | Weight | Rating guidance (1 → 3 → 5) |
|---|---:|---|
| Market / customer adjacency | 12% | Little overlap or unclear demand → adjacent segment with evidence → direct access to attractive customers/markets with validated demand |
| Capability / asset advantage | 10% | No distinctive asset → useful capability with gaps → scarce, defensible capability, technology, IP, or talent that fills a documented need |
| Strategic complementarity | 10% | Duplicative or distracts → some fit → clearly strengthens core strategy and creates specific, testable value pathways |
| Resilience and strategic risk | 8% | Concentrated/exposed business or material strategic fragility → manageable exposures → resilient position with limited strategic dependencies |

### Integration ease — 30% (higher = easier)

| Criterion | Weight | Rating guidance (1 → 3 → 5) |
|---|---:|---|
| Operating / systems compatibility | 8% | Incompatible platforms/processes → partial compatibility → compatible stack and processes; low conversion burden |
| People, culture, and retention | 7% | High attrition/conflict risk → mixed with mitigations → strong leadership/retention prospects and workable culture |
| Customer / supplier continuity | 6% | Change-of-control or churn risk is high → manageable → rights, relationships, and service continuity are well protected |
| Integration scope and time | 5% | Broad, long, interdependent transformation → moderate → narrow, staged, measurable integration plan |
| Regulatory / operational complexity | 4% | Material unresolved approvals or control gaps → manageable → limited approvals and straightforward operating transition |

### Financial attractiveness — 30%

| Criterion | Weight | Rating guidance (1 → 3 → 5) |
|---|---:|---|
| Standalone return at proposed price | 10% | Below hurdle / value depends on speculative synergies → near hurdle → comfortably above hurdle using supportable standalone cash flows |
| Cash-flow quality and durability | 7% | Volatile, weak conversion, or low visibility → acceptable → recurring, diversified, high-conversion cash flows |
| Balance sheet and funding needs | 5% | Major hidden liabilities / heavy reinvestment → manageable → sound balance sheet, modest needs, and funding fit |
| Downside resilience | 5% | Severe plausible downside destroys value → tolerable with mitigations → robust downside case and meaningful protections |
| Synergy value net of execution costs | 3% | Little supportable net benefit → plausible but uncertain → quantified, attributable, realizable benefits after costs and dis-synergies |

The small explicit synergy weight avoids letting optimistic synergy narratives dominate. The price/return criterion uses **standalone** economics; net synergies appear only in the designated synergy criterion / scenario analysis, not again in standalone return. Integration costs are reflected in synergy net value and in return scenarios only once per calculation. If your return metric already includes synergies and costs, do not add the synergy criterion’s economic value again: score it as confidence/realizability of the included case or redistribute its 3% weight.

## 4. Calculation and uncertainty

For each dimension: `dimension score (0–100) = Σ(weight in that dimension × rating ÷ 5) ÷ dimension weight × 100`. Overall: `Σ(all criterion weights × rating ÷ 5)`. With weights normalized to 100%, the overall result is 0–100. Use the same criteria and weights for all targets in a comparison.

For uncertainty, record a low/base/high plausible rating or underlying range plus confidence and evidence. A practical initial shortlist method is to rank on base case, show low-case total, and flag any target whose rank depends on low-confidence items. For a risk-averse screen, use the low rating for material unverified items; for a neutral screen, use the midpoint and show the range. Do not hide uncertainty in extra arbitrary penalties. Escalate material unknowns into a diligence condition or gate.

Separate three concepts:

- **Price:** enter the proposed enterprise/equity value consistently, bridge debt/cash and working capital, and assess standalone value and return at that price. A higher price lowers return attractiveness; it does not lower strategic fit.
- **Synergies:** list each source, accountable owner, timing, probability, one-time cost, recurring cost, dis-synergies, and dependencies. Use probability-weighted, after-tax cash flows in valuation; avoid counting revenue uplift as both a strategic premium and a second cash-flow benefit.
- **Integration costs and downside:** include one-time integration costs, stranded costs, retention, capex, and disruption in the cash-flow/NPV case. Downside resilience rates exposure and mitigations; it is not a second deduction of the same expected loss from base-case NPV. Show a separate downside scenario (e.g., slower realization, lower retention, revenue loss, cost overrun) and compare to the buyer’s hurdle.

A useful valuation bridge is: `standalone value at buyer hurdle + probability-weighted after-tax synergy NPV − integration costs − dis-synergy / disruption costs − risk-adjusted liabilities = indicated ceiling price`. Ensure integration costs or liabilities subtracted here are not also deducted a second time elsewhere in the valuation. Compare the ceiling with the proposed price; the scorecard’s return score summarizes the result rather than replacing the bridge.

## 6. Sensitivity checks

These illustrative sensitivity cases use the worked example and reweight the dimensions to 100%:

| Case | Strategic | Integration ease | Financial | Alder | Birch | Cedar | Rank |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Base | 40% | 30% | 30% | 80.8 | 79.6 | 70.8 | Alder, Birch, Cedar |
| Strategy-led | 60% | 20% | 20% | 82.4 | 78.4 | 76.8 | Alder, Birch, Cedar |
| Execution-led | 25% | 50% | 25% | 79.0 | 82.0 | 66.0 | Birch, Alder, Cedar |
| Financial-led | 25% | 25% | 50% | 79.0 | 78.0 | 67.0 | Alder, Birch, Cedar |

This shows the first-place rank changes when implementation capacity is paramount. Do further sensitivities on meaningful assumptions, not just weights: price ±10–20%; synergy realization at 0%, 50%, and 100% of base; integration cost +25–50%; realization delayed 1–2 years; major-customer churn; and downside revenue/margin cases. Translate each into return/ceiling price and re-score only criteria genuinely affected. Avoid changing both a score and the underlying cash-flow case for the same effect. Report rank reversals and the assumptions that cause them; rank stability is useful evidence, not proof of value.

## 7. Reusable scorecard

Use one row per target and criterion, with these fields: `Target | Gate status | Dimension | Criterion | Weight | Rating (1–5) | Low | Base | High | Confidence | Evidence / source | Key assumption | Owner | Open diligence question`. Score all targets at a common valuation date and information cutoff. The CSV template provides the criterion rows and information fields. Duplicate the rows per target, retaining identical weights for comparisons.

Record a short investment thesis, disconfirming evidence, gate exceptions, proposed price basis, standalone return, synergy/cost bridge, downside case, and next decision date. Keep raw source facts distinct from analyst judgment. Preserve version/date and scorer notes so changes can be traced.

## 8. What to gather before treating a target as serious

- **Strategy and market:** buyer strategy and explicit fit hypothesis; market size/growth and independent validation; competitor position; customer overlap, concentration, churn, pipeline, pricing, and win/loss data.
- **Financial quality:** audited statements (3–5 years), current monthly results, quality-of-earnings work, revenue recognition, cash conversion, working capital seasonality, capex, debt/debt-like items, leases, pensions, taxes, contingent liabilities, and forecast bridge.
- **Price and returns:** transaction perimeter, equity-to-enterprise value bridge, proposed consideration, financing assumptions, standalone cash flows, hurdle rate, return model, alternative uses of capital, and downside / break-even cases.
- **Synergies:** line-item source, baseline, owner, one-time and run-rate amounts, timing, probability, tax, dis-synergies, dependencies, customer/employee impact, and evidence that benefits are incremental and achievable.
- **Integration:** systems/data architecture, process map, cyber posture, integration leaders/capacity, people/retention risks, culture, facility footprint, operating dependencies, timeline, costs, and continuity plan.
- **Legal / regulatory:** corporate structure and ownership, material contracts/change-of-control clauses, IP chain of title, litigation, compliance, privacy, cybersecurity incidents, environmental matters, permits, antitrust/foreign investment, labor, and required approvals.
- **Commercial and operational:** customer/supplier references, service levels, backlog quality, product roadmap, key-person dependencies, supply chain resilience, insurance, safety, and business continuity.
- **People and culture:** leadership depth, compensation/equity obligations, attrition, labor agreements, succession, retention needs, and employee consultation requirements.
- **Evidence hygiene:** source, date, period, definition, and owner for each material claim; reconcile management representations to third-party evidence; flag estimates and missing records.

Advance only when the gates are clear, price is within a defensible ceiling or has a credible structure, downside fits risk appetite, and the diligence plan can resolve the few uncertainties most capable of changing the decision.
