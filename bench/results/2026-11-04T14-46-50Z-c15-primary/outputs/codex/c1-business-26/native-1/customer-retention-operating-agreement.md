# Customer Retention Operating Agreement

**Status:** Draft for Sales and Support lead review  
**Agreement owners:** Sales lead and Support lead  
**Review cadence:** Monthly for the first quarter; quarterly thereafter  
**Effective date:** On approval by both team leads

## Purpose and working rules

This agreement makes customer outcomes a shared responsibility while keeping one clearly accountable owner at each stage. Sales owns the accuracy of commitments it makes. Support owns the quality and coordination of service after handoff. Both teams own surfacing risk early, sharing evidence, and helping resolve customer-impacting issues. Neither team may close a risk or attribute churn based only on opinion.

Use the customer's desired outcome as the priority. Record material commitments, customer concerns, decisions, and next steps in the shared customer record (CRM or agreed system of record). A verbal escalation does not replace a written record.

## Assumptions to confirm

- **Renewals:** This draft assumes Sales owns the commercial renewal (forecast, terms, negotiation, and close). Support owns service health, adoption signals, open issue status, and a recommendation on renewal risk. If the company has Customer Success, assign it as renewal owner and keep the Sales/Support obligations below.
- **Roles:** “Accountable owner” means one role answerable for progress and outcome at that stage. Team leads name a delegate when unavailable. The customer-facing coordinator may differ from the person doing the technical or commercial work.
- **Systems and hours:** The teams share a customer record and escalation channel. Response targets below are elapsed business hours during the customer’s contracted support coverage, unless the account has a stricter contractual SLA. An after-hours contractual incident process takes precedence.
- **Authority:** The Sales lead and Support lead can resolve operational disputes. Material commercial concessions or policy exceptions go to the executive sponsor or designated decision-maker.

## Escalation process

### Severity and response targets

Response time means **acknowledge, name a coordinator, and communicate the next update time**. It is not a promise to resolve within that time. Targets are maximums; contractual SLAs take precedence.

| Severity | Trigger examples | Acknowledge / coordinator named | Update rhythm | Coordination and decision path |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| **S1 — Critical** | Customer cannot use a core service; broad outage/data integrity or security concern; imminent material harm or executive-level churn threat tied to an active incident. | 15 minutes during contracted coverage | At least every 30 minutes, even if there is no new fix | Support incident lead coordinates technical response and customer updates. Support lead is accountable for service response; notify both leads immediately. Security/privacy issues follow the dedicated incident process. |
| **S2 — High** | Major workflow impaired, no reasonable workaround, multiple users affected; renewal at risk within 30 days due to unresolved service issue; significant missed commitment. | 1 business hour | At least every 4 business hours | Support case owner coordinates, with a named Sales partner for account/commercial recovery. Support lead resolves service priorities; both leads agree account recovery actions. |
| **S3 — Moderate** | Limited impact or workaround exists; repeated issue or adoption blocker; credible risk to a renewal more than 30 days away. | 1 business day | Every 2 business days or on material change | Support case owner coordinates resolution; Sales joins if customer outcome, scope, stakeholders, or renewal is affected. |
| **S4 — Routine / disagreement** | How-to request, low-impact defect, ordinary scope question, or internal ownership disagreement without immediate customer harm. | 2 business days | At agreed milestone | Normal Support queue or relevant account owner. Team leads address internal disagreement under the decision process below. |

### Triggers and routing

Escalate when any of these occur: impact meets a severity definition; an agreed commitment/date is likely to be missed; a customer repeats the same unresolved concern; a renewal decision is within 120 days and health is red/declining; a champion or executive signals dissatisfaction or intent to leave; a handoff lacks a material promise or dependency; or Sales and Support disagree on ownership in a way that delays a customer action.

1. The person who sees the trigger logs it in the shared record and alerts the Support case owner and account owner (Sales) in the agreed escalation channel. Include impact, evidence, severity, customer contact, promised next update, and requested decision.
2. Support assigns the resolution coordinator for service-related risks. For a commercial-only risk, Sales coordinates. For mixed risks, Support coordinates the incident/workstream and Sales coordinates the account recovery plan; name one overall customer-facing lead so the customer receives one joined-up update.
3. The coordinator confirms severity, roles, next update, actions, owners, and dates. The coordinator keeps a decision/action log and ensures the customer gets updates at the stated rhythm.
4. Lower severity can be upgraded immediately if impact grows. Downgrade or close only when evidence supports it and the customer has been told the status and next step.

### Unresolved disagreements

Discuss disagreements using the written commitment, customer impact, case history, and agreed scope. Do not delay an urgent customer action while debating internal fault. The coordinator chooses a safe, reversible next step within their authority and records it.

- If the team owners cannot agree within **4 business hours for S1/S2**, or **2 business days for S3/S4**, escalate to both team leads.
- Team leads decide within **1 business day** (S1/S2) or **3 business days** (S3/S4), naming a decision owner, rationale, and action owners.
- If the decision requires a policy exception, unapproved commercial concession, or remains unresolved, the leads jointly escalate to the executive sponsor, who decides within 2 business days. For an active incident, the incident commander retains authority over immediate containment and technical response.
- A disagreement about cause is handled in churn review and does not suspend customer recovery work.

## Sample customer-risk scenario

**Situation:** A customer is 45 days from renewal. A key workflow is failing for several users after a configuration change. Support has an open S2 case; a workaround exists but is cumbersome. The original opportunity notes say “works with your current identity setup,” but contain no validation details. The customer’s champion says they will recommend against renewal if the workflow is not stable in two weeks.

**Response:** Support remains accountable for the technical case, names the case owner as coordinator, confirms impact/workaround, and gives the customer a next update within the S2 target. Sales owns the renewal plan and contacts the champion to clarify outcomes, decision process, and other renewal conditions; Sales does not promise a fix date. Within one business day, both leads review the original promise and technical evidence, agree a recovery milestone plan, and name one customer-facing lead. Support provides progress at least every four business hours while S2 remains active; Sales provides the commercial/stakeholder update on the agreed schedule. If the fix date is at risk, Support escalates severity/priority and presents options; the two leads decide whether an approved workaround, specialist help, or commercial recovery is appropriate. After resolution, confirm with the customer and update renewal health. If churn occurs, review whether the compatibility statement was validated, whether the handoff captured it, and whether recovery actions were timely; assess preventability from evidence rather than assigning blame from the outcome alone.

## Shared measures and anti-gaming rules

Review these together. Segment by customer size, product, renewal window, and support plan where useful; avoid ranking individuals from a single metric.

| Measure | Shared definition / use | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Gross renewal / logo retention | Renewed recurring value and retained customers among those eligible to renew in the period. | Report cohort and exclusions consistently; distinguish contraction from full churn. Sales and Support review the same denominator. |
| At-risk recovery rate | Red/declining accounts that return to agreed health or renew, divided by accounts entering the period at risk. | Freeze an “at risk” snapshot at period start; retain accounts that churn or downgrade in the cohort. |
| Handoff completeness and acceptance | Percent of handoffs with required fields and Support acceptance within 2 business days. | Missing/incorrect information is visible to both teams; do not reward fast acceptance if material gaps remain. |
| Customer outcome progress | Percent of active customers with a documented outcome and a current milestone/status; pair with customer confirmation or agreed evidence. | Do not substitute login/activity counts for achieved outcomes. Record blocked customer dependencies separately. |
| Escalation response and update reliability | Share of escalations meeting acknowledgement and promised customer update times, by severity. | Track both speed and update reliability; do not improve scores by downgrading severity without documented rationale. |
| Churn learning completeness | Share of churned accounts reviewed on time with evidence, primary reason, contributing factors, and preventability category. | Do not use raw “preventable churn” as an individual quota or punitive ranking. |

## Preventable and external churn classification

The joint review assigns one primary category and any contributing factors:

- **Likely preventable:** Evidence shows a material controllable failure or missed recovery opportunity (for example, inaccurate promise, incomplete handoff, delayed response, unaddressed adoption barrier) materially contributed, and a reasonable action could likely have changed the outcome.
- **Partly preventable / mixed:** A controllable service or relationship issue contributed alongside customer or market factors; impact cannot be cleanly separated.
- **Primarily outside control:** Evidence points to factors neither team could reasonably control, such as company closure, acquisition strategy, mandated vendor consolidation, discontinued customer initiative, or an externally imposed budget shutdown, with no material controllable failure identified.
- **Unknown / insufficient evidence:** Customer reason or timeline is unclear. Keep this category until evidence is available; do not default to “outside control.”

Use the customer's stated reason, dated commitments, case and usage history, stakeholder changes, and recovery timeline. Record confidence (high/medium/low) and what evidence is missing. The label guides process improvement; it is not a verdict on an individual.

## Review cadence and upkeep

- **Weekly (15 minutes):** Sales and Support account owners review S1/S2 risks, red renewals within 120 days, action owners, and customer update commitments.
- **Monthly (45 minutes):** Team leads review shared measures, overdue actions, handoff gaps, at-risk recoveries, and a small sample of churn reviews. Agree up to three process changes with owners and dates.
- **Quarterly:** Leads review whether targets and severity definitions fit actual coverage, update role names/system fields, and confirm renewal ownership. Publish the revised version and communicate changes to both teams.
- **After a significant churn or S1 incident:** Hold a blameless learning review within 10 business days; update this agreement only when a repeatable process change is warranted.

## Lead review checklist

- Confirm renewal owner and any Customer Success role.
- Confirm support coverage calendar, contractual SLA precedence, and escalation channel.
- Name lead delegates and executive sponsor.
- Confirm the shared system of record and required handoff/renewal fields.
- Approve targets or adjust them to match actual coverage and customer commitments.
